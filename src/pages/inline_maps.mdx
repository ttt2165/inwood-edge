# Inwood's Edge

How a remade shoreline shapes new investment, from Dyckman Street to West 218th Street.

## 1. The edge was remade

In 1924, much of this edge was vacant land, rail and water. The red line is
the 1924 shoreline. Land shaded brown on the river side of it, about 55 acres,
is made land: Sherman Creek and marsh that were filled in later. Tick
**Land use today** in the layer panel to compare. Click a lot for details.
The 1924 land use is my reading of a blurry aerial, so the groups are broad.

<Map config={edgeRemade} height="60vh" class="map-frame not-prose" />

## 2. Rules on the lots

The 2018 Inwood rezoning created the Special Inwood District (dashed purple)
and placed (E) designations on 73 lots (red). An (E) designation means a
developer must test for hazardous materials, and on some lots meet air-quality
or noise rules, before building. Most of them line 9th and 10th Avenues,
the old industrial strip near the river, and 12 overlap today's FEMA 1% flood
zone (blue). Click an (E) lot to see its rules.

<Map config={rulesOnLots} height="60vh" class="map-frame not-prose" />

## 3. Who builds where

Twenty new buildings have been filed since 2018. Every private project (black)
sits outside today's FEMA flood zone, about 1,585 homes in total. The only
housing filed on the flood line is North Cove, 611 fully affordable homes
(green). Public and nonprofit sponsors are building where private developers
are not. Click a dot for the sponsor, homes and filing date.

<Map config={whoBuilds} height="60vh" class="map-frame not-prose" />

## 4. What is at risk later

By the 2080s, the 100-year floodplain (dark dashed line) is projected to reach
well past today's FEMA zone. Inside it are 158 residential lots with about
8,990 homes (blue), and 109 public facilities. More than half of those
facilities are schools, child care and youth programs. Tick **Flood
Vulnerability Index** to see social vulnerability by census tract. Most of
these homes are in lower-score tracts, so the risk here comes more from where
things were built than from who lives there.

<Map config={atRisk} height="60vh" class="map-frame not-prose" />

These maps show where risk, rules and investment overlap. They do not by
themselves prove that the old shoreline caused today's pattern. To see every
layer together, open the [full page map](../full_page_map/).
